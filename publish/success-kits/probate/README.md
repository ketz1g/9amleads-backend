**14-email campaign · 4 letter templates · 3 flyer inserts · 4 follow-up sequences**

Here's exactly how successful probate practitioners convert probate leads.

4. Estate accounts need to be prepared for beneficiaries

I've put together a simple checklist that explains each step. If you'd like a copy, just reply and I'll send it over.

Warm regards,
[Your name]

## 📠 Flyer Insert Templates

### Probate Services Brochure (A5 Flyer)

**Front:** Gentle headline "Navigating Probate? You Don't Have To Do It Alone." with your logo.
**Inside:** List your services (grant application, estate accounts, IHT returns, asset distribution). Include testimonials from families you've helped.
**Back:** Step-by-step probate timeline infographic. Contact details.
**Tip:** Use warm colours and compassionate language. Avoid aggressive sales messaging.

### Probate Guide Flyer

**Headline:** "Probate in Plain English — A Simple Guide"
**Body:** Brief explanation of the probate process in 5 simple steps with estimated timeframes.
**Bottom:** "Need help? I'm here. Call [phone] or visit [website]"
**Tip:** This is an educational piece — families keep it for reference.

### Executor Checklist Flyer

**Headline:** "Your 10-Step Probate Checklist"
**Body:** Actionable checklist from registering the death to final distribution.
**Bottom:** "Tick each step off with professional support — free initial chat."
**Tip:** Print on high-quality paper. The checklist format makes it useful for months.

## ❓ Objection Handling

### "We're handling it ourselves"
**Response:** "Many families start that way, and that's completely understandable. If at any point you find the process overwhelming — particularly with the inheritance tax return or estate accounts — please know I'm here to help with as much or as little as you need."
**Follow up:** Send your probate step-by-step guide.

### "We're using a local solicitor"
**Response:** "That's a sensible approach. If I can share one piece of advice — make sure they specialise in probate and know the current IHT thresholds. I'd be happy to provide a free second opinion on any aspect of the estate if helpful."
**Follow up:** Email your probate FAQ sheet.

### "We need to discuss with family"
**Response:** "Of course. I've put together a simple information pack that you can share with them. It explains everything clearly — costs, process, timeline. Would you like me to post a printed copy?"
**Follow up:** Post printed information pack addressed to the family.

## ⭐ Best Practices

### Lead with compassion
Probate is emotional. Your tone must be gentle, respectful, and helpful — never pushy. Families remember how you made them feel.

### Educate, don't sell
Most people don't understand probate. Provide useful guides and checklists. Position yourself as the expert who helps, not the salesperson who pitches.

### Physical materials build trust
A professional printed guide left with the family conveys credibility. It shows you're established and serious about helping.

### Be patient
Probate decisions take weeks or months. Follow up gently over time. Families rarely buy on the first contact.

## ⏰ Recommended Timing

### Best time to send letters
Mid-week (Tuesday-Thursday). Avoid the anniversary of the death or known family occasions.

### Best time to visit
Late afternoon, 3-5pm. Be prepared to leave quietly if the family is not ready to engage.

### Email timing
Tuesday-Thursday, 10am-12pm. Keep subject lines warm and non-salesy.
