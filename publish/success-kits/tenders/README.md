# 📋 Public Tenders Success Kit

**8-email campaign · 3 letter templates · 2 flyer inserts · 4 follow-up sequences**

Here's exactly how successful contractors convert tender opportunities.

> ⚠️ **Important:** 9amLeads provides **addresses only** — we do not supply email addresses or phone numbers. You source those yourself. The most effective approach is posting a flyer + introduction letter either in person or via Royal Mail. ## 📠 Flyer Insert Templates

### Capability Statement (A4 Professional Document)

**Header:** Company logo, name, and contact details.
**Section 1:** Company overview — who you are, what you do, key differentiators.
**Section 2:** Relevant experience — 3-4 case studies with client names, contract values, outcomes.
**Section 3:** Accreditations, certifications, insurance details.
**Section 4:** Testimonials and client references.
**Tip:** Keep to 2-4 pages maximum. Procurement managers read quickly.

### Services Overview Flyer

**Front:** "Trusted [service] provider for the public sector"
**Inside:** Services offered, geographic coverage, contract value range, key clients.
**Back:** Contact details, website, company registration number, certifications.
**Tip:** Print in full colour on quality paper. This doubles as a leave-behind after meetings.

### Case Study Flyer

**Headline:** "How we delivered [project] for [client] — £[X] under budget"
**Body:** Problem → Solution → Results format with measurable outcomes and client quote.
**Bottom:** "Ready to discuss your next tender? Contact us."
**Tip:** Specific, measurable results are what procurement teams want to see.

## ❓ Objection Handling

### "We're not accepting new suppliers"
**Response:** "I understand. Would it be possible to be added to your approved supplier list for when it opens? I'd also welcome the opportunity to submit a capability statement for your records."
**Follow up:** Send capability statement for their files.

### "We already have preferred suppliers"
**Response:** "That's good to hear. If any of your current suppliers are unable to deliver, we'd welcome the opportunity to step in. I'll leave my details and capability statement with you."
**Follow up:** Re-engage every 6 months with updated capability statement.

### "The budget has already been allocated"
**Response:** "Understood. Are there any upcoming projects in your pipeline that we could be considered for? I'd be happy to provide early input at no cost."
**Follow up:** Send relevant case studies for future reference.

## ⭐ Best Practices

### Read every word
Tender documents contain critical information — evaluation criteria, deadlines, formatting requirements. Missing a detail can disqualify you.

### Provide evidence
Don't just say you can do something. Prove it with case studies, client references, and measurable outcomes.

### Submit early
Aim to submit 24-48 hours before the deadline. This avoids last-minute technical issues and shows you're organised.

### Request feedback
If you're unsuccessful, request a debrief. Procurement teams are required to provide feedback, and it's invaluable for improving future bids.

---

## ⏰ Recommended Timing

### When to start preparing
As soon as the tender is published. Many tenders have a 3-6 week window, but quality submissions take time to prepare.

### Best time to contact buyers
Early in the tender period (first 1-2 weeks). Procurement teams are more accessible before the rush before the deadline.

### Email timing
Tuesday-Thursday, 9-11am. Public sector procurement officers are most responsive mid-week mid-morning.
