How to Win More Probate Instructions: A Solicitor's Guide

SW
Sarah Williams · 15 June 2026 · 6 min read

The probate market in the UK is growing steadily, with over 270,000 grants of probate issued every year. For solicitors, each grant represents an opportunity to secure a new instruction — but only if you know how to approach the executors at the right time and in the right way. The difference between a firm that wins 40% of its probate leads and one that wins 10% often comes down to process, not price.

In this guide, we walk through the practical strategies that high-converting probate solicitors use to turn leads into instructions. Whether you are a sole practitioner or part of a larger private client team, these tactics will help you build a reliable pipeline of estate work.

1. Speed Is Everything: The First-Contact Advantage

Probate grants are published on the Gov.uk register daily. Once a grant appears, the clock starts ticking. Executors are often unsure about the next steps — they may not even know they need a solicitor. The solicitor who contacts them first, with empathy and clarity, sets the tone for the entire relationship. Aim to reach out within 24 hours of the grant being issued. Firms that call within this window report conversion rates up to three times higher than those who wait a week.

Your outreach should acknowledge the sensitivity of the situation. A templated letter that looks mass-produced will get ignored. A personalised letter or phone call that references the deceased by name and offers clear, jargon-free guidance on the next steps will be welcomed. Executors are often grieving and overwhelmed — being helpful rather than pushy is the fastest way to build trust.

2. Segment Your Approach by Estate Value

Not all probate leads are equal. A grant for a £150,000 estate with a single beneficiary requires a different approach than a £1.2 million estate with multiple beneficiaries and a complex will. High-value estates justify a more in-depth, consultative approach. For smaller estates, a streamlined fixed-fee service can be attractive to executors who want to handle things quickly without facing unpredictable legal bills.

Segmenting your leads by estate value allows you to deploy the right resource at the right time. Junior solicitors or paralegals can handle straightforward grants of probate, while your senior private client partners take on the complex, high-net-worth estates. This not only improves your conversion rate but also protects your margins.

3. Build a System, Not a Scramble

The firms that consistently win probate instructions do not rely on individual heroics. They have a system. Every lead enters a structured workflow: initial contact within 24 hours, a follow-up call at day three, a letter of engagement sent by day five, and a courtesy call at day ten if there is no response. This systematic approach ensures no lead falls through the cracks, and it gives you reliable data on what works and what does not.

Use a simple CRM or even a spreadsheet to track your probate pipeline. Measure your conversion rate by estate value, by source, and by solicitor. Over time, you will identify patterns that allow you to refine your approach and focus your energy on the leads that are most likely to convert.

4. Address the Elephant in the Room: Costs

One of the biggest objections solicitors face when pursuing probate instructions is cost. Executors are often worried about legal fees eating into the estate. The best approach is to be transparent from the outset. Offer a free initial consultation to discuss the estate and provide a clear, written quote for your services. Consider offering a fixed fee for straightforward probate applications, with an hourly rate for more complex matters.

When executors understand exactly what they are paying for and what value you provide, they are far more likely to instruct you. Many conversion-struggling firms lose instructions simply because they were not clear enough about costs at the first point of contact.

5. Leverage Your Local Presence

Probate is inherently local. Executors tend to choose solicitors who are based in the same area as the deceased or the estate. This is where targeted geographic lead generation gives you a significant advantage. By focusing on specific counties or postcode areas, you can position yourself as the local expert and build relationships with local will-writers, estate agents, and accountants who can refer estate work to you.

If you are receiving daily probate leads for a specific county, make sure your website, Google Business profile, and marketing materials all reflect that focus. Executors searching for "probate solicitor near me" are much more likely to click on a firm that feels local and accessible.

Final Thoughts

Winning probate instructions consistently is not about being the cheapest firm in town. It is about being the first to reach out, the most empathetic in your approach, and the most organised in your follow-up. Build a repeatable system, segment your leads, and focus on being genuinely helpful to executors during a difficult time. Do that, and your probate practice will grow steadily and sustainably.
