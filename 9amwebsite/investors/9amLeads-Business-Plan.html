1. The problem

Local firms lose work they never knew existed.

A homeowner decides to move. A probate grant is issued. A company is incorporated. A planning application is submitted. A council publishes a tender.

Each of these is a business opportunity worth real money to a local firm. And each one is time-critical - the firm that makes contact first wins the work, and the firm that arrives second usually gets nothing.

But the tools available to these firms are broken:

Cold calling lists are stale, over-used and legally risky. The numbers are wrong, the people have moved on, and the compliance burden falls on the caller.

Bulk data providers sell the same record to hundreds of firms simultaneously. A "lead" that arrives after 200 other firms have received it is not a lead. It is a nuisance.

Gatekeeper platforms auction the same enquiry to multiple trades at once, which turns a sales opportunity into a race to the bottom on price.

The result is that local firms pay real money for data that has already been worked - and conclude, reasonably, that lead generation does not work.

The insight

Everyone in this market sells data. Nobody sells timing.

That is the gap. And timing is not a marketing claim - it is an engineering problem. It requires finding the opportunity at source, at the moment it appears, verifying it into something a business can actually act on, and delivering it before it has been worked.

We solved it.

Why this is genuinely difficult to copy

This is not a scraper in a bedroom. Producing a mailable lead at 9am requires five separate engineering problems to be solved at once:

  1. Five independent ingestion pipelines, each with different source formats, rate limits and failure modes.
  2. A verification and enrichment layer that turns a raw record into a deliverable address with a door number. This is the unglamorous part that competitors skip - and the part customers actually pay for.
  3. An allocation engine that honours every customer's postcode areas and daily limits without over-delivering, under-delivering or double-selling the same lead.
  4. A delivery pipeline with a hard 9am deadline, self-healing watchdogs and automated recovery.
  5. A live print integration with a national mailing partner.

Copying one pipeline is a weekend's work. Copying all five, with the enrichment and the delivery guarantee, is a programme of work.

9. Competition and defensibility

Our competitors sell volume. We sell being first.

Who we compete with: bulk lead-data resellers, trade directories and gatekeeper platforms. Their weakness is structural, not accidental - they sell the same record to many buyers, so their leads are stale by definition. They cannot fix this without breaking their own business model.

Why we are hard to copy:

  1. Five independent ingestion pipelines. Each is a separate engineering problem with its own formats, limits and failure modes. Copying one is easy. Copying five is a programme of work.
  2. The enrichment layer. Turning a raw record into a mailable address with a door number is unglamorous, difficult and expensive. It is also the reason customers can act on our leads the moment they arrive.
  3. The 9am habit. A fixed daily delivery, a dashboard and integrated print create routine - and routine creates switching cost.
  4. We sell a completed action, not data. A customer who has uploaded their artwork and switched on Auto-Send has moved their entire outbound marketing into our platform.

On data sourcing: we draw on multiple independent sources per market rather than relying on any single provider, with resilience and fallback planning built in, and we are progressively building first-party data advantages.
