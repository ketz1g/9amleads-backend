# Cap Table Sketch — £25,000 for 10% (post-money £250,000)

> Sketch only — confirm the **actual legal name, Companies House number, registered address** and whether any shares exist already before issuing anything. This assumes a fresh all-founder-held company with **900 Ordinary shares** today.

## Pre-money → Post-money maths
- Post-money valuation: **£250,000**
- Pre-money valuation: £250,000 − £25,000 = **£225,000**
- Founder holds **900 shares** (100% pre).
- Investor buys **100 new shares** → total **1,000 shares** → investor = **10% post**, founder = **90% post**.
- Share price: £225,000 ÷ 900 = **£250 per share** → 100 × £250 = **£25,000**.

All investors must be in the **same share class** to keep SEIS simple (no preferential rights, no redemption, no separate "investor" class that could threaten SEIS qualifying status).

## Practical notes
1. **Valuation sanity:** £250k post for a working, shipping, 5-vertical B2B SaaS with real (if early) revenue is a defensible angel entry. Have the 12-month model ready when challenged.
2. **SEIS cap headroom:** £25k is well inside the £250k lifetime SEIS cap, so the same investors (or new ones) can add more later via the same structure.
3. **Start Up Loans interplay:** if you also take a £25k Start Up Loan (personal loan in your name), it sits **above the company cap table** — disclose it to investors, and make sure repayments come from the founder's own arrangement, not company preference.
4. **Option pool:** if you ever want an employee option pool, consider reserving e.g. 10% before the round (founder then holds 81%, investor 9%, pool 10%) — but for a £25k raise simplicity usually wins. Keep it as-is unless an investor insists.
5. **Documents to prepare (cheap, standard):** Companies House incorporation cert · memorandum & articles (Model Articles + special share provisions if any) · board resolution allotting shares · share certificates · updated PSC register · an **Investment Agreement + Subscription Agreement** (a solicitor's short-form SEIS pack is a few hundred £ and worth it for the AA + investor paperwork).
6. **Do not** issue shares until HMRC advance assurance (or EIS) is in place, and get every investor to complete the required HMRC declarations.
