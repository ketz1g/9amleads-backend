# Deck Critique — 9amLeads investor materials

> Review date: 3 Sep 2026. Sources reviewed: `pitch-deck-outline.md`, `one-pager.md`, `financial-model.md`, `investor-outreach.md`. Ordered by likely impact on a 15-minute angel conversation.

## 🔴 1. Inconsistent numbers across the three documents (fix first)
Angels cross-check the deck against the one-pager and model. Right now the three disagree:
| Metric | Pitch outline | One-pager / financial model |
|---|---|---|
| Blended ARPU | ~£110/mo | **£150/mo** |
| CAC / payback | CAC ~£150, payback **1–2 months** | CAC ~£150, payback **under 1 month** |
| 1,000 customers | **£100k/mo** subs | **£150k/mo** subs |
| Print & Post at 1,000 | £200k/mo | ~£300k/mo |

**Fix:** pick **one** set (recommend the £150/mo ARPU one — it survives scrutiny better) and use it everywhere. The £110 figure on Slide 8 of the outline is a straggler.

## 🔴 2. "Paying" vs "trial" is muddled — your single biggest credibility risk
Different files say: *"8 real paying-trial firms"*, *"8 real firms on trial"*, *"8 real trial firms"*, and the model says *"8 real firms, 50 trial accounts"*, plus *"76 accounts"*.
An angel will ask in seconds: **how many pay, how much MRR, what's the conversion rate?** Ambiguity reads as hiding weakness.
**Fix — put a clean funnel on Slide 6 and keep one definition everywhere:**
- 76 total accounts created → X on free trial now → Y paying (Starter/Pro/Enterprise) → **£Z MRR** → last-3-months revenue £A.
- If you have real paid revenue, say it loudly with bank-statement proof. If trials are unpaid, never use "paying-trial".

## 🔴 3. No revenue-to-date anywhere
The materials never state actual MRR or revenue. For a £250k post valuation, "product is done" is not proof — revenue is. Even small real numbers (e.g. £1–3k to date) make the story believable.
**Fix:** add one line + a screenshot of Stripe/Stannp history to Slide 6 and the one-pager.

## 🟠 4. The "£25k → 150–170 customers → 1,000 by month 12" jump needs a believable engine
The 12-month model leans hard on the **~£0-CAC affiliate/staff engine** to go from ~150 (funded) to 1,000 (revenue-funded). Two things are missing:
- **Proof the engine works yet**: how many active affiliates exist today, how many referrals have they generated? If it's still 0–2, investors won't extrapolate to 850 customers from it.
- **Explicit funding gap logic**: month 1–3 is funded by the £25k; from month 4 growth is paid for by ~£22k+/mo revenue. State that bridge plainly rather than implying £25k alone buys 1,000.
**Fix:** add an "affiliate engine status" line to traction, and re-label months 10–12 as target-with-reinvestment, not funded-by-round.

## 🟠 5. What exactly does £25k buy? Make the marketing plan concrete
"Digital marketing £12k" is generic. An angel wants to see the channels and the unit maths:
- e.g. £12k → Google/LinkedIn at ~£X CAC → Y customers; affiliate engine → Z; BAR/FMB trade-body channel → W; with weekly cadence and kill/scale rules.
**Fix:** one slide (or half of the Ask slide) with channel × budget × expected customers, not just a spend table.

## 🟠 6. Moat is real but under-argued
"Freshness / first contact" is your differentiator, but it needs to sound defensible:
- **Data moat:** 5 proprietary scrapers + verification pipeline + door-number enrichment is genuinely hard to copy cheaply — say it.
- **Delivery moat:** the 9am habit + dashboard + integrated Print & Post create switching cost.
- **Honest risk:** scraping depends on sources you don't own. Address it proactively (multi-source per vertical, first-party/opt-in data, API licensing, resilient fallbacks) before an investor does.
**Fix:** add a "why us / why hard to copy" line to Slides 3–4 and a one-line risk mention in Slide 10.

## 🟠 7. Single-founder risk
One founder, no visible team/advisors. Angels discount solo founders on execution capacity.
**Fix:** name 1–2 advisors/mentors (Eagle Labs, trade-body contacts) and a hiring plan for the £8k sales/conversion spend (first hire or agency by month X, or the affiliate agent model).

## 🟡 8. Market slide is assertion, not sizing
"UK removals, probate…" without TAM/SAM/SOM maths. Two-decimal credibility is cheap:
- e.g. SOM: 200k UK removal companies/solicitors/builders × £X/mo ARPU × realistic penetration. Show the "deep niche" as a *feature* (dominance before expansion), not a limit.
**Fix:** one bottom-up line per vertical + a total SOM number you'd defend.

## 🟡 9. Print & Post economics need gross margin shown
"Roughly doubles revenue" invites the question "…and the margin on that doubling?" Postage + printing costs real cash flow.
**Fix:** state gross margin per stream (subscription ~high; Print & Post ~40–50%) and note the attach-rate assumption behind "doubles ARPU" — at what % of customers mailing does doubling actually hold?

## 🟡 10. Round structure & SEIS optics
- Use **ordinary shares**, not a SAFE — a SAFE/convertible is not SEIS-qualifying. File **advance assurance** first.
- Cap table clean (founder 90% / investors 10%); show it in the data room.
- The deck never mentions SEIS. For £25k that's your biggest carrot — put "SEIS-eligible, 50% income-tax relief" on the Ask slide.
- Mind the **3-year trading-age rule** for SEIS; if you're near/over it, pivot the pitch to EIS (30%) before sending.

## 🟡 11. Closing slide numbers should match the Ask
Slide 10 says *"take 9amLeads to 200 paying customers and £100k+ run-rate"* but the Ask maths implies ~150–170 new customers from the round. Pick one destination and make every number point at it (recommend: **"£25k → 150 paying customers → ~£22k/mo subscription, ~£40k/mo with Print & Post"**).

## If you change only three things
1. **One consistent set of numbers** (ARPU £150/mo, CAC £150, payback ~1 month) across deck/one-pager/model.
2. **An honest funnel with real revenue**: 76 accounts → X trials → Y paying → £Z MRR + 3-month revenue.
3. **Say "SEIS advance assurance filed"** on the Ask slide, and show the affiliate engine's actual numbers — not just its target.

## Suggested slide order tweak
Slide 6 (traction with revenue + funnel + affiliate proof) should come **before** Slide 5 (revenue model). Investors buy proof, then model — not the reverse.
